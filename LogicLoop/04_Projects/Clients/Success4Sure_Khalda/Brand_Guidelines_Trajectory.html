01

Brand foundation

Who we are, what we stand for and how we sound. Every design decision in this book comes from this page.

Purpose

Turn ambition into a measured plan

We help students in Amman reach the scores and universities they are aiming for, with a clear path they and their parents can follow week by week.

Positioning

The academic partner between school and a global university

For ambitious students preparing for AP, EST, SAT and international programmes, and for the parents investing in them, [Name] turns a target score into a measured plan, taught to international standards.

The idea behind Trajectory

Every student starts from a baseline and is heading to a target. The brand is built on the line between the two: it always rises, it can have milestones along the way, and it always ends in one amber point, the result. The line expresses our method: diagnostic test → personal plan → mock exams → score report.

Personality
  • Confident, never arrogant
  • Precise, never cold
  • Ambitious, never pressuring
  • Modern, never trendy
Values
  • Clarity. Everyone knows where they stand.
  • Rigour. International standards, no shortcuts.
  • Belief. High expectations, held with warmth.
  • Progress. Growth is measured and shown.
  • Integrity. Honest targets, honest timelines.
Audiences
  • Students, 14–18: want a clear goal, smart help and something they are proud to follow.
  • Parents: want structure, proof and regular, honest updates.
Tone of voice

English

Short, specific, encouraging. Lead with the plan or the number. Use exam names exactly (AP Calculus BC, EST II Math, SAT).

Arabic for parents

Clear Modern Standard Arabic. Calm and respectful. Explain the plan and how progress is reported.

Arabic for students

Natural Jordanian Arabic on social media. Friendly, direct, never slang-heavy.

02

Name and logo space

The name and logo are not decided yet. These rules reserve their place so every layout made today will accept them without redesign.

Logo zone
Logo
x x x x
  • Clear space: on every side, at least x = the height of the logo's symbol (or of the capital letters if the logo is a wordmark only).
  • Placement: bottom-left on English layouts, bottom-right on Arabic layouts, top-left on documents and letterhead.
  • The logo is never the largest element on social posts and ads. The message is.
Requirements for the future logo
  • Lockups: horizontal, stacked, and symbol-only (for the profile picture and app icon).
  • Bilingual: an Arabic version drawn to match, with equal presence on signage.
  • Minimum size: symbol 24 px / 8 mm; full lockup 96 px / 25 mm wide.
  • Colour versions: full colour on Paper, reversed (Paper) on Indigo, one-colour Indigo, one-colour black for stamps and forms.
  • Fits the system: geometric construction, rounded ends matching the trajectory line, no gradients, no graduation caps or globes.
  • Name in running text: written in full in the body font, never in the logo font, never in capitals unless the name itself is an acronym.

Logo · reversed
Logo · white on blue
Logo · on photo

Bilingual rules
  • Arabic headlines at about 90% of the English size, with line height 1.4–1.5 (Arabic needs more vertical room).
  • Arabic body at line height 1.8.
  • Arabic layouts are mirrored: text, logo zone and the trajectory line all run right to left.
  • Exam names stay in Latin script inside Arabic text: SAT, EST II, AP.
  • Use Arabic-Indic digits (١٢٣) in Arabic-only designs and Western digits (123) for scores in bilingual designs, so numbers match the official score reports.
Fallbacks and don'ts
  • In Word, PowerPoint and email, where brand fonts may be missing: Arial for English and Tahoma for Arabic.
  • No more than two weights per layout per language.
  • No italics in Arabic, no outline or shadow effects on any text.
  • Never set long paragraphs in Sora or Alexandria; they are for headlines.
  • Never centre body text longer than three lines.
05

Graphic language

A small set of elements, used with discipline. The trajectory line is the signature; everything else supports it.

The trajectory line
  • Direction: always rises, left to right in English and right to left in Arabic. It never descends overall.
  • Angle: overall rise between 15° and 35°.
  • Weight: stroke = 0.6–0.8% of the layout width (7–8 px on a 1080 px post). Round ends and joins.
  • End point: one Amber circle, diameter 3× the stroke.
  • Milestones: up to 3 hollow circles at bends, for weeks, mocks or stages.
  • Frequency: one line per layout. It may cross slides in a carousel as one continuous line.
Soft square

Corner radius = 22% of the side. Used for photo frames, number tiles, buttons and the future app icon.

Score-scale pattern

Thin tick marks taken from real score scales (SAT 400–1600, AP 1–5, EST). Background texture at 15–25% opacity, never behind body text.

Icons

24 px grid, 2 px stroke, round ends, Indigo. Filled with Clear Blue only when active or selected. No 3D or emoji icons.

Charts and progress
  • Scores in Clear Blue; the target as a dashed Amber line; the latest result as an Amber point.
  • Light grid, labels in Slate, no 3D or shadows.
  • Every chart shows its scale and dates, so parents read it without explanation.
  • Example values only.
06

Photography

Real people, real study, real light. Photography shows the warmth that the colour palette alone cannot.

Subjects
  • Students concentrating, mid-problem
  • Tutors explaining at the board or beside a student
  • Hands on a mock paper, a timer, a marked test
  • Results moments: opening a score report, an offer letter
  • The branch: clean rooms, study corners, the entrance
Look
  • Soft natural window light from one side
  • Shallow depth of field; one clear subject
  • Warm skin tones, slightly cool indigo shadows
  • Space left on one side for headline and the line
  • Eye level or slightly below, which makes students look capable
Rules
  • Our own photos of the Khalda branch. No stock people.
  • Written parental consent for every student under 18, stored with the file.
  • Modest, natural styling that respects families.
  • On dark layouts, photos sit under a 60% Indigo overlay.
  • Never: flash, heavy filters, fake classrooms, posed thumbs-up.

12

Do's and don'ts

The quickest check before anything is published or printed.

Do

  • Keep one message and lots of space per layout
  • Use the trajectory line once, always rising, ending in Amber
  • Keep Amber for results and the main action
  • Use real photos of the Khalda branch with consent
  • Design Arabic layouts natively, right to left
  • Check every number before it is published
  • Leave the logo zone empty until the logo exists

Don't

  • Use graduation caps, globes, rockets, light bulbs or book stacks
  • Make the line go down, zig-zag sharply, or end in an arrow
  • Use Amber for text on light backgrounds (1.7:1 contrast)
  • Use Clear Blue for small body text on Paper
  • Add gradients, glows, shadows or 3D to text or the line
  • Use stock photos, cartoons or more than one emoji per post
  • Promise guaranteed scores or exaggerate results

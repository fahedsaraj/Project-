Starting point

What the current identity says today

@success4sure_khalda
4.5K followers · 79 posts

The current mark is a maroon serif "SUCCESS" with a graduation cap on the S, "4SURE" in grey underneath and a faint world map behind it. The bio leans on emojis ("Your pathway to top score results! 📈").

  • Keep: the promise of results, and the international-exam focus (EST, SAT, AP, IGCSE). Families already recognise the maroon.
  • Leave behind: the graduation cap, the world-map texture and the decorative serif. These are the most common tutoring-center signals in the region, so they make the academy look like everyone else.
  • The gap: parents need to see an institution they can trust with their child's university application. Students need something they would be happy to follow and share. Today the brand speaks to neither strongly.

Read from the public Instagram profile only. Posts and reels were not reviewed in depth.

Sections 1–3

Strategy shared by every direction

These three parts stay the same whichever visual direction is chosen. They are the brief the name and logo must answer.

1 · Brand personality

The mentor who has been there

Picture a young, sharp tutor who studied abroad, knows every section of the SAT and still remembers how stressful it felt. Calm, exact and encouraging. Never loud, never fussy.

  • Confident, not arrogant. Results speak; no exaggerated claims.
  • Precise, not cold. Clear plans, real numbers, warm tone.
  • Ambitious, not pressuring. Targets are motivating, never frightening.
  • Modern, not trendy. Current enough for students, stable enough for parents.
2 · Brand positioning

The academic partner between school and a global university

Positioning statement. For ambitious students in Amman preparing for AP, EST, SAT and international programmes, and for the parents investing in their future, [Name] is the academy that turns a target score into a clear, measured plan, taught to international standards by tutors who know the exams inside out.

Against: tutoring centers that sell hours, and private tutors with no system. [Name] sells a structured path with progress you can see.

Proof points to build on: diagnostic test → personal plan → mock exams → score report. Present these as the brand's method, not as extras.

3 · Core values

Clarity

Every student knows where they stand, what comes next and why. Plans and reports are simple to read, for students and parents.

Rigour

International standards in content, materials and mock exams. No shortcuts presented as strategies.

Belief

High expectations held with warmth. We push because we think the student can get there.

Progress

Growth is measured and shown: practice scores, milestones, offers received.

Integrity

Honest about timelines and realistic targets. Parents get the truth, early.

Voice in one line

Plain, specific, encouraging. English for exam and programme terms; clear Modern Standard Arabic for parents; natural Jordanian Arabic on student-facing social. Avoid hype words and stacks of emojis. Talk about plans, scores and next steps.

Instead of: "Guaranteed top scores!!! 🚀🔥"

Say: "Your target: 1450. Your plan: 10 weeks, 4 full mocks, weekly score reports."

Sections 4–8

Three visual directions

Each direction is a different answer to the same strategy. Each one has its own concept, palette (with HEX, RGB and CMYK), typography, logo direction and graphic elements, plus a sample post. Choose one, or take the structure of one and the colour of another.

CMYK values are starting conversions from screen colour. Confirm them against a printed proof and a Pantone match before the final guideline.

Direction A

Trajectory

Every student is on a path from where they are to where they want to be: a baseline score rising to a target score, school rising to university. The identity is built on that line. It is calm, confident and forward-moving, with deep indigo for trust and a sunrise amber for the moment of success.

7 · Logo direction

  • Type: a symbol plus wordmark, with the symbol able to stand alone as the app icon and profile picture.
  • Symbol: one continuous rising line ending in a point, contained in a soft square. Abstract enough to suit any name; it can also form the name's initial.
  • Wordmark: geometric sans in Title Case, medium weight, slightly tight spacing. An Arabic version drawn to match, not typed in a default font.
  • Feel: calm, exact, upward. Works at 32 px on a phone and on a 3-metre sign.

8 · Graphic elements

  • The trajectory line: a single stroke that rises left to right (right to left on Arabic-first layouts) and ends in an amber point. Used once per layout.
  • Shapes: soft squares with a 22% corner radius, echoing the symbol.
  • Pattern: a faint score-scale grid (200–800, 1–5) for backgrounds and covers.
  • Icons: 2 px line icons with rounded ends, filled only when active.
  • Educational cues: score bars, milestones and week counters instead of books and caps.
Direction B

Modern Scholar

The look of a respected international school, rebuilt for today. It keeps the maroon families already know and turns it into a deep garnet, pairs a modern serif with clean sans text, and uses a simplified seal. This direction speaks most directly to parents and to academic credibility.

7 · Logo direction

  • Type: a seal (emblem) plus wordmark, with a simplified version for small sizes.
  • Symbol: a monoline shield or circle seal holding one idea, for example an open book or the name's initial. Two strokes at most, so it does not look like an old school crest.
  • Wordmark: modern serif in Title Case, generous spacing. Arabic in a matching calligraphic-modern style.
  • Feel: established, trustworthy, quietly proud. Like a school with a 50-year history that redesigned last year.

8 · Graphic elements

  • Lines: thin brass rules and frames, as on certificates and diplomas.
  • Shapes: arches and seal circles, used as photo frames.
  • Pattern: a fine engraved texture taken from the seal, at 5% opacity.
  • Icons: thin line icons in garnet or ink.
  • Educational cues: results tables, university crests (with permission), exam calendars.
Direction C

Every student in this academy knows the bubble sheet. This direction turns it into the brand: a grid of circles where one is filled, meaning the right answer and the student who got there. It is bold, youthful and made for social media, while the clean grid keeps it disciplined enough for parents.

Recommendation

Lead with Direction A, Trajectory

It is the best balance for this audience. Parents see a serious, international institution; students see something modern they would follow. Its central idea, a measured path from baseline to target, is the academy's real method, so the brand and the service tell the same story. It also works with almost any future name, because the symbol is abstract.

A · Trajectory

Best overall balance. Strongest link to results and method. Risk: can feel corporate if photography is cold, so keep the photos warm and human.

B · Modern Scholar

Strongest with parents and keeps the current maroon. Risk: drifts traditional if serif and seal are overused. Best if the new name sounds established.

C · Answer Sheet

Strongest on Instagram and with 15–18 year olds. Risk: can feel young to parents paying for AP and university prep. Best if the new name is short and bold.

A workable mix: Direction A's structure and line, with a garnet accent from Direction B to carry the current brand's recognition through the change.

Section 9

Photography style

Photography is where an academy proves it is real. Use our own photographs of real students and tutors, taken at the Khalda branch, not stock images of smiling strangers.

What to shoot

  • Focus: students mid-problem, tutors explaining at the board, a hand on a mock paper, a parent meeting.
  • Real milestones: score reports being opened, acceptance letters, results day.
  • Details: pencils on a bubble sheet, a timer, a marked practice test, a laptop with College Board open.
  • The space: clean classrooms, study corners, the entrance with the sign.

How it looks

  • Natural window light, soft shadows, warm skin tones. No harsh flash.
  • Shallow depth of field so the student is the hero and the background stays calm.
  • Colour grading leans slightly towards the chosen palette (cool indigo shadows for A, warm ivory for B, bright and clean for C).
  • Natural, modest styling that respects families. Always get written consent from parents before photographing any student under 18.
Sections 10–11

Social media, posts and reels

Instagram is the academy's shop window, for students and for the parents who look over their shoulder. The grid should look like one consistent publication.

10 · Overall visual style

  • Content mix: 40% teaching value (tips, common mistakes, quick questions), 30% proof (results, testimonials, offers), 20% life at the academy, 10% announcements.
  • Grid rhythm: alternate photo-led and type-led posts so the grid breathes. One brand graphic element per post, never all of them.
  • Templates: 6 fixed formats (tip, mistake, result, quote, announcement, carousel), each with a set place for logo, title and programme tag (AP / EST / SAT).
  • Bilingual: Arabic and English versions as separate designs, each laid out natively (right to left for Arabic), never squeezed into one tile.
  • Highlights: Programmes · Results · Schedule · Tutors · Location, with covers in the brand palette and one icon each.

11 · Post and reel direction

  • Feed posts (1080×1350): one clear headline of up to 8 words, plenty of margin, the programme tag top left, logo small at the bottom.
  • Carousels: slide 1 is the hook; each following slide is one idea; the last slide is a clear next step (book a diagnostic test). Keep the graphic line or grid continuous across slides.
  • Reels (1080×1920): hook in the first 2 seconds (a question on screen, a real student reaction, a timer). Captions burned in, in the brand fonts, inside the safe zone. End card with the symbol.
  • Formats that suit the brand: "60-second question", tutor tips to camera, mock-exam day behind the scenes, student journey from baseline to final score.
  • Motion: elements move the way the concept moves: the line draws upward (A), the seal fades in (B), a bubble fills in (C).
Section 12

Advertising design direction

Principles

  • One message per ad: one programme, one intake, one action.
  • The hero is a real student or a real number ("Average SAT gain: +170", only when the academy can prove it).
  • Hierarchy: headline → proof → action. The logo is visible but never the biggest thing.
  • Parent-targeted ads lead with structure and results. Student-targeted ads lead with the goal and the feeling.

Formats

  • Meta feed 1080×1350, stories and reels 1080×1920, Google display sets.
  • Intake campaigns (summer, winter, before each exam date) built from one master design, then adapted.
  • Every claim (scores, rankings, university offers) is checked and documented before it runs.
Section 13

Print and stationery

Business cards

Symbol alone on the front in the primary colour; name, role, phone and QR code on a light back. Uncoated, heavy stock.

Letterhead and reports

Logo top left (top right in Arabic versions), thin rule, wide margins. The monthly progress report is a key brand piece: a clear score chart for parents on one page.

Study materials

Branded booklets and mock-exam covers with programme colour codes (for example AP, EST and SAT each get one shade from the secondary palette).

Certificates

The most formal application. Direction B's brass rule style can serve here even if A or C is chosen.

Merchandise

Notebooks, pencils and tote bags with the symbol only. Students should want to carry them.

Brochure

One per programme, A5, photo-led cover, with a clear path: diagnostic, plan, mocks, result.

Section 14

Signage and physical academy branding

A family's first visit decides a lot. The branch should feel like a calm, organised place of study, not a shopfront.

Outside

  • Main sign: wordmark in Arabic and English with equal presence, lit at night, on a solid primary-colour panel.
  • Window graphics: frosted band with the graphic element (line, seal or bubbles) for privacy and identity.
  • Check municipality signage rules in Amman before production.

Inside

  • Reception wall: large symbol on the primary colour, with the positioning line in both languages.
  • Classrooms named or numbered with consistent door signs; programme schedules in branded frames.
  • A results wall that is updated every exam season (with consent).
  • Wayfinding in bilingual pictograms, same icon set as social media.
Section 15

Do's and don'ts

Do

  • Use real students, tutors and results.
  • Keep one main message and lots of space on every design.
  • Use the accent colour only for achievement and action.
  • Design Arabic layouts natively, right to left.
  • Show the method: diagnostic, plan, mocks, report.
  • Check every number and claim before publishing.
  • Keep the logo's clear space and minimum size.

Don't

  • Use graduation caps, globes, rockets, light bulbs or stacks of books as decoration.
  • Use cartoon characters, bubble fonts or rainbow colours (too childish).
  • Use stock photos of people who are not at the academy.
  • Promise guaranteed scores or use exaggerated claims.
  • Fill posts with emojis, stickers or more than two fonts.
  • Stretch, recolour or add effects (shadows, glows, 3D) to the logo.
  • Mix the three directions' elements in one layout.

Next steps

From direction to finished brand

  1. Choose a direction (or a mix) with the academy.
  2. Name: develop the new name. The chosen direction will guide which names fit.
  3. Logo: 3 concepts in the chosen direction, in Arabic and English.
  4. Full guideline: final palette (with Pantone), type licences, logo rules, templates and mock-ups.
  5. Rollout plan: Instagram switch-over, signage, print, and how to tell current families about the change.

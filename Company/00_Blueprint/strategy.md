# Strategic Foundation: United International Academy

> v1.0, 7 Oct 2026. Atlas's working strategy. It combines Fahed's brief, the Brand Guidelines v1.0 and the 7 Oct social/ads audit. **Hypotheses are marked.** It gets replaced by data as the 4-week baseline arrives.

## 1. What we really sell
- **To parents: certainty.** Someone who understands the international system their child is in, so the investment in an international education pays off.
- **To students: control and confidence** before the exam, and a clear path to the university they want.
- The guidelines frame this as **"a clear path beyond graduation"** and **"Shaping Global Minds."**

## 2. Value proposition (draft)
> *One academy that understands every international system your child studies in, with teachers who know the exams, a structured plan, and results you can see, for grades 10–12.*

It rests on three supports:
1. **System fluency:** American, British and IB.
2. **Structure:** a plan, not ad-hoc sessions.
3. **Proof:** documented results and clear communication with parents.

## 3. Audience & segments
- **Who decides:**
  - parents decide and pay, and judge on trust
  - students discover us and can veto, and judge on relatability and the teachers
  - peers, school WhatsApp groups and teachers' reputations drive referrals
- **Segment by:**
  - **System:** American / British (IGCSE) / IB
  - **Need state:**
    - *exam crunch:* SAT, EST II, AP finals
    - *year-round support*
    - *rescue:* falling behind
    - *ambition:* top universities
  - **Audience:** student or parent, the same offer told two ways
- **Highest-value family (hypothesis):** enters in grade 10 with year-round support and stays through AP/IB and SAT/EST II. Siblings make it more valuable still.
- **Priority now:**
  1. Existing and past families (retention + referrals).
  2. Parents of grade 10–12 students in Amman international schools with an exam within 6 months.
  3. Grade 10 students starting the journey.

## 4. Programme architecture
| Layer | Programmes | Role | Message |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Exam products** (seasonal, high intent) | SAT, EST II, AP (+ ACT, IELTS, TOEFL once D-03 confirms) | Acquisition: they bring families in | "Your target score, with a plan and teachers who know the exam." |
| **System support** (recurring) | IGCSE, IB, American/British school subjects | Retention and lifetime value | "We know your curriculum." |

Ads lead with one or two exam products, timed to their exam windows. Support programmes are sold to families already in the funnel.

## 5. What the audit tells us (7 Oct 2026; full data in `02_Reference/social_ads_audit_2026-10-07.md`)
1. **Audience mismatch:**
   - Only 41% of Instagram followers are in Amman.
   - 70% are aged 18–34.
   - Only 14% are 13–17 and 15% are 35–54.
   - A +4,960 follower spike on 25–27 Aug, followed by steady losses, suggests low-quality followers. Organic reach goes to people who won't enroll.
2. **Formats that work:**
   - Reels average 1,095 reach vs. 251 for static posts.
   - The best performers feature real people, a parent's point of view or emotional exam moments.
   - The weakest are batches of per-subject flyers (13 in one day, ~130–200 reach each).
3. **No measurable lead pipeline:** ~106 (ad-account currency) bought 29 WhatsApp conversations; the leads campaign recorded 0 form leads, and no enrollments can be traced.
4. **Brand inconsistency:**
   - 7+ versions of the name
   - 2 phone numbers
   - rolling discounts
   - unverified claims ("نتائج مضمونة", "80% scored 5", "leading AP centre in the Middle East")
   - a live post with placeholders

## 6. Competitive advantages (to validate against named competitors)
- Several international systems under one roof
- Khalda location
- Existing families, teachers and results
- In-house creative production (Fahed): most local academies post weak content
- The academic kit as a physical brand touchpoint

## 7. Risks to the rebrand
- Families not realising it's the same team. Mitigation: personal outreach first, same number, "formerly" line if D-01 allows.
- Scaling ads before tracking and fast follow-up. Mitigation: the build order.
- Inconsistent application of the new identity. Mitigation: templates, QA gate, touchpoint audit.
- Over-promising. Mitigation: banned-claims list; the Integrity value.
- Calling UIA a "school" without a licence. Mitigation: D-02.
- The classroom experience not matching the premium promise. Mitigation: flag it to management, and collect feedback.

## 8. Single strategic focus
> **Trust that converts:** one consistent brand, backed by visible proof, feeding a lead pipeline where every qualified parent is captured, answered fast, and followed through to enrollment.

## 9. Metrics
- **North star:** enrollments from marketing-sourced leads.
- **Pipeline:**
  - qualified leads/week
  - cost per qualified lead
  - median first response (< 15 min target)
  - lead → trial
  - trial → enrollment
- **Retention:**
  - existing-family retention through the transition
  - re-enrollment
  - referral share
- **Brand:** touchpoint consistency, 100% by the end of P01.
- **Secondary:** reach and engagement.

## 10. The academic kit (school bag)
- Position it first as the **enrollment welcome kit**:
  - tangible proof of a premium service
  - students carry the brand into schools
  - natural unboxing content
- Later, add programme kits (a SAT kit, an IGCSE revision kit) as paid products.
